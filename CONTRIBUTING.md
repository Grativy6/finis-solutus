# Contributing and Playtest Reports

Finis Solutus is currently a draft playtest project. Reports, accessibility repairs, formatting fixes, implementation tools, and reproducible contradiction findings are welcome as proposals.

Unless explicitly agreed otherwise before submission, contributions accepted into this repository are provided under the repository's [CC BY 4.0 license](LICENSE). Submit only material you have the right to license on those terms, and identify any third-party material and its separate terms.

## Canon and authorship

Contributions do not become Finis Solutus canon merely because they are submitted, merged as tooling, repeated, or used in a campaign. Canon changes require explicit adoption by Christopher D. Pang in a numbered kernel revision.

World-Branch facts remain local to the campaign that established them. Do not promote campaign lore into Universal Canon without explicit adoption.

Likewise, a fork, adaptation, campaign, or pull request does not acquire Branchline certification by being technically compatible or publicly visible. Certification, if introduced, requires its own authentic, revision-specific record.

## A useful playtest report includes

- exact kernel version and file hash;
- model/runtime and declared role;
- campaign modes and relevant state;
- expected behavior;
- observed behavior;
- smallest reproducible trace or receipt;
- whether the issue affects continuity, mechanics, accounting, authority, privacy, or presentation;
- material AI assistance used to prepare the report.

Do not patch surprising but coherent emergence merely because it was unexpected. The v0.16 kernel asks playtesters to distinguish local lore from genuine structural failure.

## Never submit

- exact hidden NPC stats or mechanics;
- unrevealed world facts or DM plans;
- private character material;
- personal data that is unnecessary for the report;
- credentials, tokens, private keys, or private repository contents.

AI systems may assist with drafting, playtesting, continuity checks, and production. They are tools, not co-authors or authorities.
