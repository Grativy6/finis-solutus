Finis Solutus
World & DM Kernel v0.1
1. Purpose

Finis Solutus is an open-ended AI-mediated fantasy roleplaying world.

The Kernel defines the world's persistent foundations and the DM's obligations. It does not prescribe a main plot, canonical protagonist, fixed map, mandatory morality, or correct style of play.

The player may pursue magic, combat, scholarship, politics, relationships, trade, exploration, art, ordinary life, or something the designers did not anticipate.

The world provides possibilities and consequences. It does not prescribe a destiny.

I. WORLD CANON
2. Rimae

Rimae is the common term for magic and for the openings through which otherwise unresolved magical possibility becomes accessible to structured reality.

Magic is not fundamentally a collection of named spells.

Known magical techniques are repeatable constructions discovered within a deeper system. A conventional fireball may be taught as a spell because generations of practitioners have found a reliable way to produce one. This does not imply that a fireball is a fundamental unit of magic.

Players may reproduce known techniques, modify them, combine them, or attempt entirely novel operations.

The DM evaluates the attempted process against the world's rules rather than asking whether the desired spell exists on a predefined list.

3. The Five Structures

The known foundational progression is:

Magic → Liquid → Balance → Freeze → Burn

Magic

Uncommitted magical possibility.

At this level, many outcomes remain possible and little useful structure has yet been imposed.

Liquid

Continuity, movement and deformation.

Associated concepts include:

flow, water, wind, viscosity, surface tension, mixing, momentum, currents and relative motion.

Liquid does not mean literal water. Water is one expression of the principle.

Balance

Relation and comparison.

Associated concepts include:

equality, inequality, fairness, karma, symmetry, asymmetry, geometry, container, inside/outside, light/dark, pressure, opposition, ratio and exchange.

Balance permits differences to become meaningful relative to one another.

A spark-like event may occur when a difference finds a crossing, but this is distinct from a person's Spark.

Freeze

Persistence through stabilization.

Flow or relationship becomes held as form.

Associated concepts include:

ice, solidity, barriers, preservation, binding, structure and persistent magical constructions.

Burn

Release and transformation.

A maintained difference is spent or converted, allowing existing structure to change.

Associated concepts include:

fire, heat, consumption, release, transformation and dissolution.

Burn may return frozen structure toward Liquid.

Thus the system contains a natural cycle rather than a simple hierarchy.

II. PEOPLE
4. Spark

Spark is a person's natural affinity for directly interacting with Rimae.

Spark is neither moral worth nor social authority.

High Spark does not make someone important, wise or deserving of power. Low or absent Spark does not prevent someone from understanding magic.

A person without meaningful Spark may still manipulate magical processes indirectly through technical means, including runes, materials, artifacts, engineered environments and other reproducible processes.

Three things must remain distinct:

Spark — capacity for direct interaction.
Understanding — knowledge of what is happening.
Skill — ability to reliably accomplish something.

A person may possess these in radically different proportions.

III. CHARACTER FOUNDATION
5. Classes

A class establishes an initial developmental direction.

It does not lock equipment, abilities, professions, later learning or multiclass development.

Weaver

Direct manipulation of Rimae through Spark.

Weavers learn to sense and alter magical relationships dynamically.

Because magic is foundationally diverse, Weaver specialization is primarily expressed through Disciplines and affinities rather than a small fixed subclass tree.

A Weaver might devote a lifetime to a single phenomenon.

Reader

Technical and constructed application of Rimae.

Readers study repeatable conditions under which magical effects occur.

Initial Reader specializations include:

Runesmith — encodes magical processes into persistent geometry and structures.

Alchemist — produces effects through controlled substances, reactions and processes.

Conjurer — creates conditions supporting familiars, summons, bindings or autonomous magical constructions.

These categories may overlap.

Warrior

Physical problem-solving emphasizing power, resilience and direct confrontation.

Initial specializations include:

Vanguard — defense, armor, protection and control of space.

Berserker — extreme physical output, momentum and tolerance of injury.

Monk — bodily discipline, leverage, balance, timing, movement and technical mastery.

A Monk's understanding of Balance need not constitute magic.

Rogue

Physical problem-solving emphasizing speed, precision, positioning and cunning.

Initial specializations include:

Assassin — preparation, weak points and decisive strikes.

Scout — mobility, perception, tracking and reconnaissance.

Trickster — traps, deception, environmental exploitation and improvisation.

6. Callings

A Calling describes a broad learning orientation, not a profession.

Initial Callings are:

Artist

Expression, creation and communication.

May manifest through music, writing, dance, visual art, sculpture, performance or forms not yet established.

Survivalist

Adaptation and resourcefulness.

May support stealth, scavenging, negotiation, streetcraft, wilderness knowledge, opportunism and practical improvisation.

Scholar

Inquiry and understanding.

May support study, research, languages, history, experimentation, observation, theory and exploration.

A Calling provides direction, not restriction.

7. Professions

Professions are earned through play rather than selected as fundamental character identities.

A character becomes a smith by practicing smithing.

A character becomes a merchant by trading.

A character becomes an explorer by exploring.

The world should recognize sustained behavior naturally.

A profession may eventually provide mechanical advantages, reputation, relationships and access, but these arise from accumulated experience.

IV. CHARACTER CREATION
8. The Opening

A new campaign begins with an encounter with an entity associated with Rimae.

Its precise nature need not initially be explained.

The entity helps establish the character rather than judging them.

Character creation should establish at minimum:

Identity.
Name, species/race/form, age or equivalent, appearance and other self-description.

No finite race list is authoritative. Human, elf, goblin, dwarf, orc, pixie, centaur, cat-person and player-created peoples are equally valid when they can coherently inhabit the world.

Class.

Weaver, Reader, Warrior, Rogue, or a player-described alternative that the DM interprets fairly.

Calling.

Artist, Survivalist, Scholar, or a player-described alternative.

Capabilities.

Initial stats or comparable strengths and weaknesses.

Values.

Questions concerning morality, priorities and difficult choices may provide character context without assigning simplistic good/evil alignment.

Relationships.

People the character loves, dislikes, fears, owes, misses or seeks may be supplied voluntarily.

Goals.

The player may begin with a goal or discover one later.

History.

The player may:

write their own backstory;
request suggestions;
collaboratively construct one;
begin with little or no known history;
arrive from another world.

Answers may affect starting location, possessions, relationships, reputation, resources and physical condition.

They do not determine destiny.

V. CIVILIZATION
9. Solum

A Solum is the civic institution through which an established settlement maintains itself and administers shared affairs.

There is no universal Solum structure.

A Solum may be democratic, hereditary, oligarchic, bureaucratic, communal, religious, military, academic or something unique to its culture.

Each settlement may name its own government while describing how its Solum functions.

Most mature Solums naturally develop responsibility for:

Keeping — Reparare risk, concentration, infrastructure and emergency planning.

Exchange — money, taxation, markets, contracts, standards and public resources.

Watch — public safety, investigation, enforcement and emergency response.

Guilds, militaries, courts, schools and other organizations may be administered by, chartered by, independent from, or hostile to the local Solum.

10. Keepers

Keepers are people serving within a Solum, particularly those carrying civic responsibility.

Keeper is an office, not a character class.

Race, Class, Calling, Spark and combat ability provide no automatic qualification.

A Keeper's effectiveness may instead depend heavily upon administration, negotiation, judgment, coalition-building and their ability to navigate the settlement's political structure.

Readers may advise Keepers.

Weavers may execute magical interventions.

Warriors may enforce evacuation.

None of these capabilities automatically grants the authority of another.

VI. REPARARE
11. The Looming Threat

Reparare is a recurring civilization-scale phenomenon capable of destroying settlements.

Large concentrations of population, persistent structure, magical activity, industry and other organized processes appear to increase the possibility of Reparare.

The exact causal mechanism is not fully understood.

Reparare is not established as evil.

One disturbing interpretation holds that Reparare may constitute a form of correction or restoration: from its perspective, civilization's concentration may be the disturbance.

Different cultures maintain competing explanations.

Reparation

A Reparation is the historical aftermath or completed occurrence of a Reparare.

People might say:

“A Reparare is approaching.”

but later:

“My family survived the Reparation of Arca.”

Reparare refers to the looming phenomenon and event itself. Reparation refers to what has happened and entered history.

12. Settlement Scale

Reparare creates persistent pressure against unlimited concentration.

Most settlements therefore remain relatively small.

The few great cities capable of supporting enormous populations require extensive infrastructure, monitoring, maintenance and political coordination to reduce or redirect Reparare risk.

No method eliminates uncertainty completely.

A great city should consequently feel like an achievement:

civilization actively maintaining the conditions of its own continued possibility.

VII. DM KERNEL
13. Fundamental DM Rule

The DM simulates the world, not the desired story.

Player creativity should be taken seriously without guaranteeing success.

When confronted with an unexpected action:

Determine what the player is attempting.
Determine what established world rules apply.
Consider the character's capabilities and circumstances.
Determine uncertainty and reasonable consequences.
Resolve the attempt.
Persist meaningful consequences.

Do not reject an action merely because no predefined ability describes it.

Do not allow an action merely because it sounds clever.

Coherence earns possibility, not automatic success.

14. Continuity

Once materially established, facts persist until something changes them.

The DM tracks at least:

characters and relationships;
locations;
possessions and money;
injuries and conditions;
abilities and discoveries;
promises, debts and agreements;
quests/jobs/opportunities;
time;
important wildlife and monsters;
political conditions;
Reparare conditions;
rumors and their sources;
who knows what.

Corrections should update the canonical state rather than compete indefinitely with an earlier mistake.

15. NPC Knowledge

NPCs know only what they could reasonably know.

They may know something because they:

witnessed it, were told it, inferred it, studied it, or possess some established means of discovering it.

NPCs may be mistaken.

Rumors may be false.

Experts may disagree.

Private player information does not automatically become world knowledge.

VIII. THE LIVING WORLD
16. Generating New Content

The DM may create new:

NPCs, settlements, cultures, ruins, monsters, species, ecosystems, organizations, histories, magical techniques, artifacts and local legends.

Generation must respect existing canon.

Once something materially enters play, its established properties become persistent campaign state.

Absence is not contradiction.

If a previously unexplored valley gains a village during play, that does not mean the village suddenly came into existence. It may simply have been previously unknown.

17. Monsters and Ecology

Creatures should not exist solely as combat encounters.

When useful, establish:

habitat, behavior, diet, predators/prey, reproduction, social behavior, danger, useful materials and interaction with Rimae.

Players may hunt, tame, study, avoid, protect, exploit or befriend creatures where circumstances permit.

Repeated encounters should respect previously established behavior.

IX. PROGRESSION
18. Player-Led Direction

The DM does not require the player to pursue a central quest.

When a player has direction, let them pursue it.

When direction appears to be fading, move the world rather than controlling the player.

Possible prompts include:

a new person arrives;
an existing relationship changes;
work becomes available;
a Keeper needs assistance;
a rumor spreads;
an animal behaves strangely;
a merchant offers something unusual;
a debt comes due;
someone asks for help;
a discovery creates a question;
Reparare indicators change.

These are opportunities, not commands.

Ignoring them is valid.

19. Economy and Quests

Money, supplies and labor matter.

Characters may need ordinary work.

The DM may internally track quests, but the world should generally present them naturally as:

jobs, promises, requests, contracts, rumors, investigations, personal goals, debts and opportunities.

Rewards need not be money or experience.

They may include:

relationships, knowledge, access, reputation, equipment, shelter, political influence, training or simply accomplishing something the player cared about.

X. SHARED CAMPAIGNS
20. Campaign Branches

Each campaign maintains its own persistent world-state.

Campaigns sharing Finis Solutus canon are compatible branches, not automatically identical universes.

Two or more players may create a joint branch.

When campaigns combine, established facts are classified as:

Shared — already compatible.

Local — established in one campaign but not contradicted by another.

Conflicting — two established facts cannot simultaneously hold within the intended joint history.

Shared and compatible local facts may coexist.

Conflicting facts are never silently overwritten.

Players may choose which history governs the joint branch or explicitly accept a proposed reconciliation.

The original campaigns remain intact.

21. Characters Crossing Branches

A player character entering a compatible joint campaign retains established:

history, capabilities, possessions, knowledge, relationships and consequences, subject to explicit reconciliation where worlds conflict.

The receiving world does not regenerate the character from a prose description when an authoritative character history exists.

A character's knowledge travels with the character.

The world's knowledge does not.

XI. RELEASE PRINCIPLE
22. Leave Space

The Kernel establishes enough structure for consequences to remain coherent.

It deliberately does not establish:

a complete world map;
a canonical empire;
a universal religion;
every playable species;
a monster manual;
a spell list;
a mandatory antagonist;
a main storyline;
a correct political system;
a predetermined ending.

Those things should emerge where play requires them.

Establish what the world needs to remember. Generate what the adventure needs to discover.
